How a report is placed

By the desk’s own rules: the ones in causes.ts that the cause table on #4471 already runs, plus the desk’s own notes.

  • one-day: the desk read the report as a one-day move (desk-notes.json). The booking moved by a day, not by a month: “Tuesday 6 October showed as Monday”. A parser that reads the month first swaps day and month; it cannot move a date by one day. This is a separate bug, most likely a time-zone conversion: one report has the same meeting on Tuesday in Lisbon and on Monday in Toronto. Like the cause table on #4471, the table above keeps them counted under parser, where they are filed, and footnotes them there; one-day tracks them as a problem of their own.
  • hint: the tenant runs booking-v3 and the report came in through the form. The form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field, people type the month first, and the form reads the date the tenant’s way, day first. Fixing the parser does not change the hint.
  • parser: every report from a tenant whose locale writes the day first, asked of the platform as causes.ts does, that did not come through a booking-v3 form. The shared parser reads the date month first.

The tenant decides the cause, not the reporter’s browser. Intake’s tag is a guess made from the browser locale; the notes under the table count where it guessed wrong.

Why they were merged

Decision 0001 merges reports whose subject lines match. All of these problems arrive under the same subjects: “Wrong day on the invite” and “Month and day swapped?” each carry reports of every problem above. Matching on the subject cannot keep them apart. The explorer on triage shows the same thing: the same subject is not the same cause.

What changes for the desk

  • Ship the parser fix against its own ticket. The hint and one-day tickets stay open after it ships.
  • Send new reports from a booking-v3 form, and reports of a one-day move, to their own tickets, not to #4471.
  • Decision 0001 needs a successor record that places reports by tenant, form and channel, not by subject. That is a new record, signed by its deciders; 0001 stays as it is.
